# NMZ Serratia explainer: speaker notes

12 main slides followed by 8 optional backups. Main talk: approximately 18–20 minutes.

## 1. NMZ Serratia genomics

About 1 minute. SM-NMZ came from slaughterhouse effluent. C14-NMZ came from the Calotropis gigantea rhizosphere in Baluran National Park, East Java. This talk explains what the genomic analyses add to the collaborative study. The leading findings concern SwrW-associated serratamolides, pigment-associated genes and extracellular-matrix loci. Aromatic metabolism and a putative LadA-like protein add comparative context. Keep any final oil-performance claim tied to the experimental team's actual material and results. Source: final Bakta v1.12.0/database v6.0 summary, JSON and FASTA for SM-NMZ and C14-NMZ. Genome properties are reported in manuscript Table 1.

## 2. DNA, gene predictions and activity

About 1½ minutes. A read is a recorded DNA fragment. Assembly combines overlapping evidence into reconstructed sequences. Annotation predicts where genes occur and what their products may do. The genome is a parts list. It does not say which parts are active in a particular experiment. A related protein can use a different substrate, and a present gene can remain unexpressed. The experimental sample might contain cells, a supernatant or an extracted fraction, each with different biological content. An activity assay measures the effect of the material actually tested. The project has no expression measurement reported in these genomic analyses. Source: final Bakta v1.12.0/database v6.0 summary, JSON and FASTA for SM-NMZ and C14-NMZ. Genome properties are reported in manuscript Table 1.

## 3. What each kind of evidence answers

About 1½ minutes. Use swrW as the example. Protein similarity locates a plausible biosynthetic candidate. Gene context can show whether associated machinery is organized coherently. LC-HRMS supplies chemical evidence at its actual identification confidence. A tension measurement would show an interfacial effect for a specified material. A controlled genetic disruption or purified-compound experiment can test a particular contribution. These sources answer different questions. Several annotation programs can reuse the same reference information, so apparent agreement is not automatically independent confirmation. Reference-strain causality should remain separate from what was measured in NMZ. Sources: focal antiSMASH Region 11, original SwrW clinker figure, and manuscript biosynthesis results. Shanks et al. (2012), https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0036398, provides reference-strain genetic/chemical evidence, not an NMZ perturbation experiment.

## 5. SwrW and reported serratamolides

About 2 minutes. The image is a crop of the original clinker figure showing only the focal pair. The orange arrows mark swrW. Arrow direction follows gene orientation; ribbons connect protein matches. Their presence does not demonstrate transcription. The focal SwrW proteins share 98.9% amino-acid identity and carry a Ser-selective C–A–PCP–TE architecture. Characterized SwrW uses one module to make a cyclic dimeric depsipeptide, so one module does not imply a one-amino-acid product. The architecture supports family-level biosynthetic potential and does not select an exact congener. The manuscript reports LC-HRMS serrawettin W1 and serratamolide C for both isolates under the author's working assumption. W1 is also called serratamolide, so aliases are not independent discoveries. ## 6. Extracellular matrices and surface activity

About 2 minutes. EPS means extracellular polymeric substances, which can include polysaccharides and other material. A capsule remains associated with the cell, while the composition of released material depends on growth and extraction. Both isolates have capsule, EPS2 and cellulose-associated loci. The displayed DNA Features Viewer panel shows the important focal difference: a contiguous pgaDCBA locus in SM, compared with corresponding C14 flanks lacking that intervening locus. C14 still has a separate pgaB homolog elsewhere. The pga system is associated with PNAG, a matrix polysaccharide, but no polymer chemistry or amount is measured here. Large polymers can influence emulsion stability, while small amphiphiles can affect interfaces. Their relative roles depend on which material the assay uses. The HADEG Emulsan category contains generic capsule markers. Source: figures/neighborhoods/neighborhoods.json and plotted-features.tsv, generated from the final-size Bakta annotations. DNA Features Viewer: https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btaa213. PNAG reference: https://doi.org/10.1128/JB.186.9.2724-2734.2004.

## 7. Aromatic metabolism depends on the substrate

About 2 minutes. Aromatic refers to ring chemistry. A hydrocarbon contains only carbon and hydrogen. Phenylacetate and hydroxyphenylacetate contain oxygen, so these aromatic substrates are not hydrocarbons. The shared hpa locus shown here and the paa locus support organized candidate routes for those particular compounds. SM additionally has hydroxybenzoate/protocatechuate and gentisate-associated candidates, with some downstream assignments still unresolved. These differences suggest experiments with specified substrates. They do not establish a faster rate or petroleum-wide degradation. The HADEG Toluene_degradation hit corresponds to a hydroxybenzoate-hydroxylase candidate in a pcaK neighborhood, rather than a complete toluene pathway. ## 8. The first alkane oxidation remains unresolved

About 1½ minutes. This is a simplified aerobic terminal-oxidation route. It does not describe all possible alkane pathways. Initial oxidation converts an alkane to an alcohol and requires appropriate initiation machinery. Downstream alcohol/aldehyde processing and fatty-acid metabolism cannot establish that initial reaction. The annotations named alkB in these genomes are DNA-repair demethylases, a different protein from membrane alkane hydroxylase AlkB. The reported searches did not resolve a convincing initiation system. This negative result is bounded by the methods, reference coverage and thresholds used. If controlled experiments show a chemical transformation, report that observation while keeping the initiating enzyme unresolved. An emulsion or lower pour point alone does not show alkane oxidation. Source: focal annotation products, HADEG tables and manuscript search results. Source: LadA-analysis/07_phylogeny/LadA_smartgap.treefile and .iqtree. ## 10. Matching assays to genomic candidates

About 2 minutes. No numerical results for these four endpoints were supplied to the genomics author. This table is an interpretation framework. Tension requires the actual interface and units, usually mN/m. E24 is emulsion-layer height over total liquid-column height after 24 hours, multiplied by 100, under a specified oil and mixing protocol. Pour point is a temperature endpoint under a stated method and thermal history. Conversion needs the exact substrate and controls for recovery, adsorption or redistribution, preferably with products or carbon accounting. A shared swrW locus cannot explain an isolate difference by itself. Regulation, abundance and mixture composition are testable explanations rather than results. A published serrawettin effect in another strain supplies precedent only. Source: manuscript four-theme passages and integration notes. Zhang et al. (2021), another strain: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00449-021-02625-4.

## 11. Host-interaction genes and application context

About 1 minute. VFDB similarity finds reference-associated homologs under a stated search procedure. ShlA and ShlB are associated with hemolysin machinery, Has with heme acquisition, and T6SS with interbacterial competition in characterized systems. Multiple database labels may describe the same genomic locus, so a hit count is not a virulence score. These findings matter if deployment is proposed, but cannot establish pathogenicity or safety of the focal isolates. Shanks et al. also links serratamolide to hemolytic activity in a reference strain. A biosurfactant label is therefore a functional description, with no automatic safety implication. Do not claim NMZ hemolysis without a relevant assay. Source: focal VFDB profiling and manuscript comparative section. Shanks et al. (2012): https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0036398. Murdoch et al. (2011): https://doi.org/10.1128/JB.05671-11.

About 1 minute, then discussion. The TYGS and GTDB results differ in their species assignment for C14-NMZ. The most useful next discussion is which experimental endpoint leads the paper and what material generated that endpoint. Match those results to these candidate systems before attributing a mechanism. Source: revised master and both concise manuscripts, TYGS report, and the focal gene-neighborhood sidecars.
