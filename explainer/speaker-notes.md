## 2. DNA, gene predictions and activity

About 1½ minutes. A read is a recorded DNA fragment. Assembly combines overlapping evidence into reconstructed sequences. Annotation predicts where genes occur and what their products may do. The genome is a parts list. It does not say which parts are active in a particular experiment. A related protein can use a different substrate, and a present gene can remain unexpressed. The experimental sample might contain cells, a supernatant or an extracted fraction, each with different biological content. An activity assay measures the effect of the material actually tested. The project has no expression measurement reported in these genomic analyses. Source: final Bakta v1.12.0/database v6.0 summary, JSON and FASTA for SM-NMZ and C14-NMZ. Genome properties are reported in manuscript Table 1.

## 4. What each kind of evidence answers

About 1½ minutes. Use swrW as the example. Protein similarity locates a plausible biosynthetic candidate. Gene context can show whether associated machinery is organized coherently. LC-HRMS supplies chemical evidence at its actual identification confidence. A tension measurement would show an interfacial effect for a specified material. A controlled genetic disruption or purified-compound experiment can test a particular contribution. These sources answer different questions. Several annotation programs can reuse the same reference information, so apparent agreement is not automatically independent confirmation. ## 5. Genes, neighborhoods, clusters and operons

Read a gene arrow as an annotated DNA feature: its position and direction are information, rather than a measurement of expression. A neighborhood means nearby genes. A biosynthetic cluster is a set of nearby genes implicated in making a molecule, with stronger evidence when machinery and known biology fit together. An operon is a transcriptional unit; mere proximity is insufficient to establish one. The pig comparison shows an organized multi-gene pigment-associated region. Its flanking genes are not all automatically part of the pigment pathway. swrW provides a useful contrast: one approximately 3.94-kb gene encodes a single Ser-selective C–A–PCP–TE NRPS module. A single module can be used iteratively, and a large predicted region is not necessarily a large experimentally delimited operon. The red/pink culture phenotype supports a pigment-associated finding without adding a chemical confirmation of prodigiosin. ## 8. ANI and digital DNA–DNA hybridization

ANI is average nucleotide identity in the genome regions that a method aligns. It is not the percentage of the whole genome that is identical, nor a confidence score for a species name. Alignment fraction describes how much sequence aligns under the particular method. dDDH is a computational estimate of DNA–DNA hybridization relatedness, calibrated to the historical experimental measure. The conventional roughly 95–96 percent ANI and 70 percent dDDH boundaries are broad species guides; they are not universally interchangeable. GTDB uses a species-specific representative radius and an AF rule. TYGS uses type-strain comparisons and dDDH clustering. The NMZ direct dDDH result uses d4; d5 distances are separately used for the tree. ## 9. GTDB-Tk and TYGS workflows

GTDB is a reference taxonomy/database; GTDB-Tk is the local classification workflow. A genome FASTA enters an ANI/AF comparison against species representatives. Genomes meeting the representative-specific ANI radius and AF criterion may be classified by the ANI screen, as both NMZ queries were. Other genomes proceed through marker-gene identification, alignment and reference-tree placement; that branch was not used for the focal classifications. The documented project run was GTDB-Tk 2.7.2 with GTDB r232; current documentation may describe a newer tool release. Type strains anchor names; a GTDB representative need not be the nomenclatural type. ## 12. EPS, CPS and matrix polysaccharides

EPS is used here for extracellular polymeric substances: a broad matrix that may contain polysaccharides, proteins, extracellular DNA and other components. Some papers use EPS more narrowly for exopolysaccharides; define the usage rather than treating every EPS label as a chemical identity. CPS means capsular polysaccharide, emphasizing a cell-associated layer. PNAG is a specific beta-1,6-linked N-acetylglucosamine polymer, often partly deacetylated, associated with pga machinery in characterized systems. Cellulose is a distinct beta-1,4-linked glucose polymer. The NMZ pga and bcs annotations support candidates; they do not determine polymer amount, exact modification or the chemistry of the assayed preparation. HADEG uses wzb/wzc capsule-associated markers under the Emulsan category, but these regulatory/export-associated homologs do not identify a particular polymer product. ## 17. LadA and the alkane-oxidation hypothesis

LadA is the name given to a soluble flavin-dependent protein originally reported to initiate long-chain n-alkane oxidation in Geobacillus thermodenitrificans NG80-2. Explain the proposed terminal oxidation as an alkane becoming a primary alcohol, before downstream aldehyde and fatty-acid processing. The label long-chain covers study-specific substrate ranges rather than one universal boundary. It motivated a search because a plausible initial oxidation step would help interpret hydrocarbon-metabolism hypotheses in this project, while downstream fatty-acid machinery alone cannot identify that step. Sequence similarity and placement with canonical LadA are reasonable candidate-discovery evidence, not a measurement of the NMZ substrate. The next slide explains why the original functional assignment now requires caution. ## 18. LadA evidence across the literature

Feng et al. 2007 reported in-vivo and in-vitro evidence for canonical NG80-2 LadA. Boonmak et al. 2014 examined B23 homologs and reported alkane depletion in a heterologous bacterial host; distinguish that evidence from a purified-enzyme assay, and do not present its non-significant 30-degree growth comparison as successful complementation. Mireles et al. 2025 tested canonical LadA, an optimized variant and four homologs and could not detect direct long-chain n-alkane hydroxylation under their in-vitro conditions despite extensive troubleshooting. They leave a possible role in the broader pathway open while questioning the first hydroxylation step. This history explains both why a homolog search was motivated and why the NMZ proteins remain candidates. It does not establish a substrate for every member of the family. Sources: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0609650104 ; https://doi.org/10.1007/s00792-014-0636-y ; https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0332702 .

## 19. Initial alkane oxidation

About 1½ minutes. This is a simplified aerobic terminal-oxidation route. It does not describe all possible alkane pathways. Initial oxidation converts an alkane to an alcohol and requires appropriate initiation machinery. Downstream alcohol/aldehyde processing and fatty-acid metabolism cannot establish that initial reaction. The annotations named alkB in these genomes are DNA-repair demethylases, a different protein from membrane alkane hydroxylase AlkB. The reported searches did not resolve a convincing initiation system. This negative result is bounded by the methods, reference coverage and thresholds used. If controlled experiments show a chemical transformation, report that observation while keeping the initiating enzyme unresolved. An emulsion or lower pour point alone does not show alkane oxidation. ## 21. Matching assays to genomic candidates

About 2 minutes. No numerical results for these four endpoints were supplied to the genomics author. This table is an interpretation framework. Tension requires the actual interface and units, usually mN/m. E24 is emulsion-layer height over total liquid-column height after 24 hours, multiplied by 100, under a specified oil and mixing protocol. Pour point is a temperature endpoint under a stated method and thermal history. Conversion needs the exact substrate and controls for recovery, adsorption or redistribution, preferably with products or carbon accounting. A shared swrW locus cannot explain an isolate difference by itself. Regulation, abundance and mixture composition are testable explanations rather than results. A published serrawettin effect in another strain supplies precedent only. Source: manuscript four-theme passages and integration notes. Zhang et al.
