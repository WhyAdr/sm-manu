## 1. NMZ Serratia genomics

SM-NMZ came from slaughterhouse effluent. C14-NMZ came from the Calotropis gigantea rhizosphere in Baluran National Park, East Java. This talk explains what the genomic analyses add to the collaborative study. The leading findings concern SwrW-associated serratamolides, pigment-associated genes and extracellular-matrix loci. ## 2. DNA, gene predictions and activity

A read is a recorded DNA fragment. Assembly combines overlapping evidence into reconstructed sequences. Annotation predicts where genes occur and what their products may do. The genome is a parts list. It does not say which parts are active in a particular experiment. A related protein can use a different substrate, and a present gene can remain unexpressed. The experimental sample might contain cells, a supernatant or an extracted fraction, each with different biological content. An activity assay measures the effect of the material actually tested. The project has no expression measurement reported in these genomic analyses. Source: final Bakta v1.12.0/database v6.0 summary, JSON and FASTA for SM-NMZ and C14-NMZ. Genome properties are reported in manuscript Table 1.

## 4. What each kind of evidence answers

Use swrW as the example. Protein similarity locates a plausible biosynthetic candidate. Gene context can show whether associated machinery is organized coherently. LC-HRMS supplies chemical evidence at its actual identification confidence. A tension measurement would show an interfacial effect for a specified material. A controlled genetic disruption or purified-compound experiment can test a particular contribution. These sources answer different questions. Several annotation programs can reuse the same reference information, so apparent agreement is not automatically independent confirmation. ## 6. ANI and digital DNA–DNA hybridization

ANI is average nucleotide identity in the genome regions that a method aligns. It is not the percentage of the whole genome that is identical, nor a confidence score for a species name. Alignment fraction describes how much sequence aligns under the particular method. dDDH is a computational estimate of DNA–DNA hybridization relatedness, calibrated to the historical experimental measure. The conventional roughly 95–96 percent ANI and 70 percent dDDH boundaries are broad species guides; they are not universally interchangeable. GTDB uses a species-specific representative radius and an AF rule. TYGS uses type-strain comparisons and dDDH clustering. The NMZ direct dDDH result uses d4; d5 distances are separately used for the tree. ## 7. GTDB-Tk and TYGS workflows

GTDB is a reference taxonomy/database; GTDB-Tk is the local classification workflow. A genome FASTA enters an ANI/AF comparison against species representatives. Genomes meeting the representative-specific ANI radius and AF criterion may be classified by the ANI screen, as both NMZ queries were. Other genomes proceed through marker-gene identification, alignment and reference-tree placement; that branch was not used for the focal classifications. The documented project run was GTDB-Tk 2.7.2 with GTDB r232; current documentation may describe a newer tool release. TYGS is a web server using genome input, nearby type-strain selection, GBDP distances, dDDH estimates and a genome phylogram. Type strains anchor names; a GTDB representative need not be the nomenclatural type. Different references and operational boundaries explain why species labels can differ without an assembly being wrong. The actual C14 outputs are retained: GTDB S. nematodiphila, TYGS S. marcescens. C14 ANI to the marcescens representative is 96.82 percent, below its 96.86 percent radius; its nematodiphila representative match is 98.22 percent, AF 0.932. ## 8. Genes, neighborhoods, clusters and operons

Read a gene arrow as an annotated DNA feature: its position and direction are information, rather than a measurement of expression. A neighborhood means nearby genes. A biosynthetic cluster is a set of nearby genes implicated in making a molecule, with stronger evidence when machinery and known biology fit together. An operon is a transcriptional unit; mere proximity is insufficient to establish one. The pig comparison shows an organized multi-gene pigment-associated region. Its C14-NMZ label follows GTDB, as explained on slide 5. Its flanking genes are not all automatically part of the pigment pathway. swrW provides a useful contrast: one approximately 3.94-kb gene encodes a single Ser-selective C–A–PCP–TE NRPS module. A single module can be used iteratively, and a large predicted region is not necessarily a large experimentally delimited operon. The red/pink culture phenotype supports a pigment-associated finding without adding a chemical confirmation of prodigiosin. ## 9. Genome-wide BGC mining with antiSMASH

antiSMASH v8.0.4 scans each closed chromosome using rule-based profile HMMs for biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs). Both SM-NMZ and C14-NMZ carry 11 chromosomal BGC regions, all internal to the chromosome (contig_edge=False). Region numbers are assigned sequentially from the chromosomal origin (dnaA), so a shared region number does not imply orthology. Nine region calls are shared across both genomes, while two regions per isolate are unique: SM-NMZ uniquely carries Region 2 (NRPS-like A–PCP–TD reductase) and Region 7 (a 2-module NRPS), whereas C14-NMZ uniquely carries Region 3 (ranthipeptide) and Region 7 (a yersinopine-like opine metallophore). In addition, Region 10 encodes completely different NRPS architectures in the two isolates. When evaluating any region, we distinguish three comparison tabs: KnownClusterBlast and MIBiG comparison compare against curated reference BGCs in MIBiG, whereas ClusterBlast searches general GenBank genome assemblies for syntenic homologs. Source: manuscript Section 3 and data/antiSMASH/antiSMASH-region-comparison.md.

## 11. Region 11: core SwrW domains vs. flanking window hits

Zooming into the Region 11 detail panels answers two common questions when reading antiSMASH outputs. First, why does the NRPS/PKS domains panel list four genes (NNLMDO_021205, 021255, 021280 and 021300) when SwrW is a single-gene synthetase? Because antiSMASH scans the entire 43.9-kb region window for any distant pHMM domain hit: NNLMDO_021205 is the only core NRPS (C_Starter–A–PCP–TE), whereas NNLMDO_021255 is a flanking housekeeping 3-oxoacyl-ACP reductase (fabG, whose short-chain dehydrogenase fold triggers a weak KR hit, E = 7.1e-10), NNLMDO_021280 is a flanking zinc alcohol dehydrogenase (adhP/yahK, triggering a weak ER hit, E = 1.5e-11), and NNLMDO_021300 is a boundary aromatic aminotransferase (tyrB, triggering an AmT hit). Second, switching to the ClusterBlast tab confirms that the entire 43.9-kb neighborhood—including the core SwrW synthetase (100% identity, 100% coverage to Serratia marcescens BVG90_RS18855)—is collinear across complete S. marcescens chromosomes. Source: SM-NMZ antiSMASH report (#r1c11) and Bakta annotations.

## 12. Why Region 11’s 0.69 MIBiG score misleads vs. Region 5 (pig)

Comparing the MIBiG comparison (Protocluster to Region) panels for Region 11 and Region 5 illustrates the central BGC lesson of our manuscript: a moderate composite similarity score does not identify a pathway. In Region 11 (left), the top MIBiG row is BGC0002286.3 (“ririwpeptide A/B/C” from Photorhabdus laumondii) with a similarity score of 0.69 (0.688 in JSON: id = 0.49, order = N/A, components = 0.67). Only a single vertical line connects the two clusters because a single core NRPS gene (NNLMDO_021205) aligns partially to plu3123 at 46.7% identity and 45.9% coverage—and five other unrelated lipopeptides (icosalide, xenotetrapeptide, gamexpeptide, xenematide, rhizomide) score 0.67–0.68 for the exact same reason (generic C–A–PCP–TE domain homology). ## 13. Catecholate siderophore potential: Regions 1 and 9

Both SM-NMZ and C14-NMZ carry two conserved catecholate siderophore-associated regions: Region 1 (called as NRP-metallophore; NRPS, 48.3 kb) and Region 9 (called as NRPS, 44.0 kb). In both regions, the core genes encode classic catecholate siderophore assembly and transport components—2,3-dihydroxybenzoate-AMP ligase (entE), catecholate NRPS modules (entF / entF2), an MbtH-like NRPS chaperone (mbtH), and an enterobactin/siderophore exporter (entS). In the MIBiG comparison tables (shown here for SM-NMZ), these conserved catecholate building blocks produce partial 5- to 6-protein matches (47–78% identity) against multiple reference clusters (pyreudione, enterobactin, trichrysobactin, serratiochelin and viobactin). Because only the shared catecholate core genes match—rather than an entire species-specific tailoring pathway—the manuscript reports Regions 1 and 9 as catecholate siderophore biosynthetic potential rather than assigning a single named siderophore product. Source: manuscript Section 3 and data/antiSMASH/SM-NMZ.tsv.

## 15. Same region number, different NRPS machinery: Region 10

Region 10 is the sharpest biosynthetic contrast between the two focal isolates and demonstrates why ordinal region numbers must be compared at the domain level. In SM-NMZ (left, 51.3 kb), Region 10 is a complex multi-gene NRPS/acyl-loading cassette: it encodes a standalone alpha/beta-hydrolase (AbH, NNLMDO_014990), a standalone type-II thioesterase (TE, NNLMDO_015030), a free-standing adenylation protein (A[Thr], NNLMDO_015035), a two-module NRPS (NNLMDO_015060: C–A[Val]–PCP–C–A[Ser]–PCP), a hybrid co-enzyme A ligase / NRPS module (NNLMDO_015065: CAL–ACP–C–A[Leu]–PCP), and a malonyl-CoA acyltransferase/carrier protein (NNLMDO_015080: PKS_AT–ACP). ClusterBlast shows 97–100% identity across ~30 collinear proteins to the Serratia marcescens UENF-22GI Gap1 locus. In C14-NMZ (right, 45.8 kb), Region 10 is an unrelated single-module D-Val NRPS (OJMJKD_018290: C–A[Val]–PCP–E–TIGR01720–C_DCL) located near a T6SS neighborhood. ## 20. EPS, CPS and matrix polysaccharides

EPS is used here for extracellular polymeric substances: a broad matrix that may contain polysaccharides, proteins, extracellular DNA and other components. Some papers use EPS more narrowly for exopolysaccharides; define the usage rather than treating every EPS label as a chemical identity. CPS means capsular polysaccharide, emphasizing a cell-associated layer. PNAG is a specific beta-1,6-linked N-acetylglucosamine polymer, often partly deacetylated, associated with pga machinery in characterized systems. Cellulose is a distinct beta-1,4-linked glucose polymer. The NMZ pga and bcs annotations support candidates; they do not determine polymer amount, exact modification or the chemistry of the assayed preparation. HADEG uses wzb/wzc capsule-associated markers under the Emulsan category, but these regulatory/export-associated homologs do not identify a particular polymer product. ## 23. Aromatic metabolism depends on the substrate

Aromatic refers to ring chemistry. A hydrocarbon contains only carbon and hydrogen. Phenylacetate and hydroxyphenylacetate contain oxygen, so these aromatic substrates are not hydrocarbons. The shared hpa locus shown here and the paa locus support organized candidate routes for those particular compounds. SM additionally has hydroxybenzoate/protocatechuate and gentisate-associated candidates, with some downstream assignments still unresolved. These differences suggest experiments with specified substrates. They do not establish a faster rate or petroleum-wide degradation. The HADEG Toluene_degradation hit corresponds to a hydroxybenzoate-hydroxylase candidate in a pcaK neighborhood, rather than a complete toluene pathway. ## 25. LadA and the alkane-oxidation hypothesis

LadA is the name given to a soluble flavin-dependent protein originally reported to initiate long-chain n-alkane oxidation in Geobacillus thermodenitrificans NG80-2. Explain the proposed terminal oxidation as an alkane becoming a primary alcohol, before downstream aldehyde and fatty-acid processing. The label long-chain covers study-specific substrate ranges rather than one universal boundary. It motivated a search because a plausible initial oxidation step would help interpret hydrocarbon-metabolism hypotheses in this project, while downstream fatty-acid machinery alone cannot identify that step. Sequence similarity and placement with canonical LadA are reasonable candidate-discovery evidence, not a measurement of the NMZ substrate. ## 26. LadA evidence across the literature

Feng et al. 2007 reported in-vivo and in-vitro evidence for canonical NG80-2 LadA. Boonmak et al. 2014 examined B23 homologs and reported alkane depletion in a heterologous bacterial host; distinguish that evidence from a purified-enzyme assay, and do not present its non-significant 30-degree growth comparison as successful complementation. Mireles et al. 2025 tested canonical LadA, an optimized variant and four homologs and could not detect direct long-chain n-alkane hydroxylation under their in-vitro conditions despite extensive troubleshooting. They leave a possible role in the broader pathway open while questioning the first hydroxylation step. This history explains both why a homolog search was motivated and why the NMZ proteins remain candidates. It does not establish a substrate for every member of the family. ## 27. Initial alkane oxidation

This is a simplified aerobic terminal-oxidation route. It does not describe all possible alkane pathways. Initial oxidation converts an alkane to an alcohol and requires appropriate initiation machinery. Downstream alcohol/aldehyde processing and fatty-acid metabolism cannot establish that initial reaction. The annotations named alkB in these genomes are DNA-repair demethylases, a different protein from membrane alkane hydroxylase AlkB. The reported searches did not resolve a convincing initiation system. This negative result is bounded by the methods, reference coverage and thresholds used. If controlled experiments show a chemical transformation, report that observation while keeping the initiating enzyme unresolved. An emulsion or lower pour point alone does not show alkane oxidation.
