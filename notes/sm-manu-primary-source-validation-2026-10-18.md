# Primary-source validation for the sm-manu genomics audit

Date: 2026-10-18
Scope: independent literature and nomenclature check for the LadA, SwrW/serratamolide, petroleum-chemistry, and species-name claims. This note is evidence for the manuscript audit; it does not edit the manuscript or the three review documents.

## Findings that should drive the revision

| Topic | Agree | Revise | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| LadA | Keep the manuscript's caution that a LadA-like annotation is not proof of alkane oxidation. | Acknowledge the historical direct biochemical and heterologous-complementation reports, then explain that a recent independent re-test did not reproduce direct hexadecane hydroxylation. Restrict the negative result to tested proteins and conditions. | “Mireles et al. disproved every LadA function” or “all previous LadA evidence is invalid.” |
| SwrW | A conserved single-module `swrW` locus supports a plausible serrawettin W1/serratamolide biosynthetic hypothesis. | Separate locus-level biosynthetic potential from product-level identification. ## LadA: historical activity and the 2025 re-test

The historical evidence is experimental, not just annotation. Feng et al. (2007) reported that purified NG80-2 LadA (GT3499) converted C15–C36 alkanes to corresponding primary alcohols, detected by GC/GC-MS. They also reported partial restoration of growth on C15–C17 alkanes when `ladA` was expressed in a *Pseudomonas fluorescens* `alkB` knockout; it did not restore growth on C6–C14. The same study observed LadA induction in hexadecane-grown NG80-2. The authors disclosed a financial conflict related to a patent application covering the `ladA` sequence; this is relevant context, not grounds to dismiss the reported results. Boonmak et al. (2014) reported growth/complementation evidence for three B23 LadA-type genes in an `alkB`-deficient *Pseudomonas* host; that is in-vivo/heterologous evidence, not purified-enzyme kinetics. These results support historical involvement in alkane-associated phenotypes, but they do not by themselves establish each homolog's catalytic step or substrate range. [Feng et al. 2007, DOI 10.1073/pnas.0609650104](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0609650104); [Boonmak et al. 2014, DOI 10.1007/s00792-014-0636-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00792-014-0636-y).

Mireles et al. (2025) provide a material challenge to the direct-monooxygenase interpretation. They tested canonical NG80-2 LadA, a more soluble engineered LadA:P5 variant, and four purified representatives selected from a wider set of homologs. The canonical enzyme did not yield detectable hydroxylated product or hexadecane consumption in their re-tests; the optimized system included purified *E. coli* Fre to regenerate FMNH₂, and they tested at 37°C and 60°C. The four purified homologs and LadA:P5 were likewise inactive under the reported assays. They sampled at time zero and two hours, used pentadecane as an internal standard, compared EI mass spectra with libraries, and matched retention times with standards; reactions had at least two technical replicates using two independently purified protein batches. Their optimized tests used a substitute surfactant because the surfactant in the older reports was no longer available. The authors also note that the earlier methods did not state enzyme concentration and did not describe an FMN-reductase source where FMN and NAD(P)H were supplied. The historical protocols were therefore re-tested as described in the papers, but exact reproduction is limited by their incomplete reporting. [Mireles et al. 2025, full paper and supporting information, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0332702](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0332702).

The 2025 results directly weaken the claim that the tested LadA proteins catalyze the first n-alkane oxygenation step under those conditions. They do not erase the earlier phenotypes, rule out a downstream or regulatory role, demonstrate that every untested homolog is inactive, or establish that all group C flavoprotein monooxygenases lack alkane activity. Mireles et al. themselves retain a possible connection between LadA and alkane metabolism while concluding that its specific role remains unresolved. Do not describe controls as absent unless the supporting information has also been checked; the main paper explicitly documents time-zero sampling, an internal standard, retention-matched standards, technical replication, and separate protein preparations.

**Suggested manuscript wording:** “The genomes encode LadA-like group C flavoprotein monooxygenase homologs, but sequence similarity does not establish alkane oxidation. Earlier studies reported alkane-associated expression, purified-enzyme activity for NG80-2 LadA, and heterologous complementation by B23 LadA-type genes. A 2025 independent study could not detect hexadecane hydroxylation by NG80-2 LadA, an engineered variant, or four purified homologs using re-tested and optimized assays. Thus, direct initial n-alkane oxidation by the focal homologs has not been demonstrated and should not be inferred from annotation alone.”

For the focal isolates, say “LadA-like candidate” or “LadA-family homolog” unless their sequence placement supports a more specific designation. Keep the DNA-oxidative-demethylase trap and the absence of a convincing canonical AlkB/CYP153/AssA-MasD initiator as separate genome observations. Neither downstream β-oxidation nor the presence of an unrelated flavoprotein establishes the missing first oxidation step.

## SwrW and the serratamolide congener claim

Li et al. (2005) characterized `swrW` as a **putative** aminolipid synthetase. Its predicted C–A–T–TE architecture contains one L-serine-selecting NRPS module (T is the thiolation/carrier-protein domain, commonly described as PCP). The paper proposed biosynthetic steps for serrawettin W1 by analogy to gramicidin S synthetase; it did not demonstrate the full catalytic cycle in vitro. A single module can therefore support a W1-family biosynthetic hypothesis, but does not uniquely predict its acyl-chain lengths or establish a specific congener. [Li et al. 2005, DOI 10.1111/j.1348-0421.2005.tb03734.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1348-0421.2005.tb03734.x).

Shanks et al. (2012) provide much stronger functional evidence for SwrW-dependent serratamolide production in their *S. marcescens* strain: `swrW` disruption eliminated the surfactant-zone phenotype, complementation restored related phenotypes, and the product was purified and characterized by HR-MS/NMR as serratamolide. The characterized W1/A product is the C10+C10 congener, reported near *m/z* 515.5. This validates the gene-product relationship in that experimental background; it does not identify the metabolite in SM-NMZ or C14-NMZ. [Shanks et al. 2012, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0036398](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0036398).

Serratamolide C is a different congener. Clements et al. (2021) distinguish serratamolide A/serrawettin W1 at [M+H]⁺ *m/z* 515.3331 (C10+C10) from serratamolide C at [M+H]⁺ *m/z* 543.3644 (saturated C10+C12), with reported UPLC retention and MSᴱ fragments. Those data support their study's assignment; an exact mass by itself in another isolate does not prove the same structure. [Clements et al. 2021, DOI 10.3389/fchem.2021.633870](https://doi.org/10.3389/fchem.2021.633870).

**Suggested wording:** “The conserved single-module `swrW` locus supports biosynthetic potential for serrawettin W1/serratamolide-family aminolipids. LC–MS features were putatively annotated as [named congener] based on [actual evidence]; the supplied records do not establish isolate-specific structural confirmation.” Insert a congener name only when the observed ion, adduct, retention behavior, diagnostic fragments, and library/reference evidence support it. MSI chemical-analysis guidance recommends transparent reporting of identification evidence and confidence; authentic-standard matching and orthogonal evidence support stronger structural assignments. [Sumner et al. 2007, DOI 10.1007/s11306-007-0082-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11306-007-0082-2).

For the colleague presentation, define C–A–T/PCP–TE plainly: A selects/activates an amino acid; the carrier domain holds the activated building block; C forms a bond; TE releases or cyclizes the product. Then explain that one predicted module is a biosynthetic clue, not a chemical identification. The two serines in W1 and the proposed iterative assembly mechanism deserve a short “model, not proven mechanism” qualifier.

## How to connect the genomic results to the wet-lab assays

The project's `hpa`, `paa`, `pob`, `pca`, and gentisate-related genes point to candidate metabolism of **named oxygenated aromatic compounds**: 4-hydroxyphenylacetate, phenylacetate, p-hydroxybenzoate/protocatechuate, and gentisate. Primary pathway work establishes those substrate contexts: [Prieto et al. 1996, hpa/4-HPA, DOI 10.1128/jb.178.1.111-120.1996](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.178.1.111-120.1996); [Ferrández et al. 1998, paa/phenylacetate, DOI 10.1074/jbc.273.40.25974](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.273.40.25974); [Doten et al. 1987, pca/protocatechuate, DOI 10.1128/jb.169.7.3168-3174.1987](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.169.7.3168-3174.1987); [Sphingomonas gentisate dioxygenase study, DOI 10.1128/jb.180.16.4171-4176.1998](https://doi.org/10.1128/jb.180.16.4171-4176.1998). These genes are not evidence that a strain initiates degradation of unmodified petroleum alkanes; nor do they alone show degradation of aromatic petroleum hydrocarbons such as BTEX or PAHs. Those require substrate-appropriate initial-activation evidence and/or direct experiments.

Treat these annotations as pathway **candidates** until pathway completeness and gene-function confidence are reviewed. Project-level annotation review supplied for this audit flags no named `pcaC` in the immediate SM `pca` region and a distal carboxymuconolactone-family candidate; the gentisate cassette's downstream predicted enzyme names do not yet clearly resolve the pathway endpoint. Do not label either route complete or experimentally functional without resolving these annotations. (This sentence records an internal project check supplied by the audit lead, not a claim from the cited pathway papers.)

Use the three measurement terms distinctly:

| Endpoint | What it measures | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Surface tension | Force per length at a liquid–air interface, commonly reported in mN/m under defined conditions. A lower value is evidence of a surface-active sample. | Stable oil–water emulsification, hydrocarbon transformation, or crude-oil flow at low temperature. Oil–water interfacial tension is also a separate measurement. |
| Emulsification (often E24) | Under a specified mixing protocol, the emulsion-layer height as a fraction of total liquid-column height after a stated settling period (often 24 h); a proxy for emulsion formation/persistence. | Chemical breakdown or mineralization of the oil. Emulsification can increase apparent dispersion/bioavailability without proving catabolism. |
| Pour point | A standardized low-temperature flow/handleability index for the crude oil, sensitive to thermal history; ASTM D5853-24 is the crude-oil-specific method and reports upper/lower pour-point procedures. | Surfactant activity or biodegradation, unless measured in a separate controlled pour-point test. |

Zhang et al. (2021) is a relevant *Serratia* precedent: their distinct *S. marcescens* S-1 strain produced serrawettin W1, lowered water surface tension, showed emulsion activity, and was associated with crude-oil hydrocarbon degradation including long-chain alkanes and aromatic hydrocarbons. That makes a bridge from a biosurfactant phenotype to hydrocarbon availability biologically plausible, but it is not evidence for SM-NMZ/C14-NMZ, their product identity, or a pour-point effect. The correct bridge is: genomic loci suggest candidate functions; the matched wet-lab data demonstrate only the measured outcomes for the tested isolate and conditions. [Zhang et al. 2021, DOI 10.1007/s00449-021-02625-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00449-021-02625-4); [ASTM D5853-24](https://doi.org/10.1520/D5853-24).

Project chemistry review supplied for this audit indicates the current slide deck contains library-style serratamolide A–E/stephensiolide annotations and two analysis workflows, but no mapping of those features to SM-NMZ versus C14-NMZ. Until the raw spectra, standards/reference records, and strain mapping support a more specific claim, retain “putatively annotated features” and do not assign a congener to a specific isolate.

## Taxonomy and threshold language

LPSN currently lists *Serratia nematodiphila* as validly published and the correct species name, with type strain DSM 21420 (also CGMCC 1.6853/KCTC 22130). It lists *S. marcescens* as a validly published correct name. *S. marcescens* subsp. *sakuensis* is also validly published at subspecies rank, but LPSN currently treats it as a heterotypic synonym of *S. marcescens*; the subspecies page notes that *sakuensis* would be the correct name if retained as a distinct subspecies. Do not call it a distinct species. [LPSN: *S. nematodiphila*](https://lpsn.dsmz.de/species/serratia-nematodiphila); [LPSN: *S. marcescens*](https://lpsn.dsmz.de/species/serratia-marcescens); [LPSN: *S. marcescens* subsp. *sakuensis*](https://lpsn.dsmz.de/subspecies/serratia-marcescens-sakuensis).

The 2018 minimal-standards proposal gives commonly accepted species-boundary guides of approximately 95–96% ANI and 70% dDDH for genome comparisons, ideally against the relevant type strains. They are operational guidelines, and the paper explicitly notes continuing scientific discussion; they are not a nomenclatural rule that automatically chooses one name when a query lies above threshold to more than one named taxon. [Chun et al. 2018, DOI 10.1099/ijsem.0.002516](https://doi.org/10.1099/ijsem.0.002516).

For C14, the project audit reports that GTDB-Tk release v2.7.2/r232 assigns *S. nematodiphila*, while the TYGS/dDDH comparisons also cross conventional species thresholds to *S. marcescens*. Report the GTDB assignment as one explicit genome-taxonomy result and the TYGS overlap as a difference between operational species boundaries. The original run uses a 96.86% radius for the marcescens representative, and C14's recorded 96.82% ANI to it is below that radius; applying a universal 95% cutoff would obscure this distinction. A concise manuscript form is: “GTDB-Tk (r232) assigned C14 to *S. nematodiphila*; TYGS returned above-threshold dDDH to both *S. nematodiphila* and *S. marcescens* type/reference genomes, so the assignment is reported with this genome-based ambiguity.” Confirm the accession/type-strain mapping and the exact TYGS/GTDB inputs in the methods or supplement before finalizing that sentence. These C14 figures are from the project audit supplied to this note, not independently recalculated here.

## Additional bibliography check

The primary Frontiers page for CANT-HYD gives the title “CANT-HYD: A Curated Database of Phylogeny-Derived Hidden Markov Models for Annotation of Marker Genes Involved in Hydrocarbon Degradation,” DOI [10.3389/fmicb.2021.764058](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2021.764058), with publication date 7 January 2022 and volume 12 (2021). It is a curated marker-gene annotation resource; it does not substitute for experimental validation of a focal strain's predicted pathway. If this source appears in the manuscript bibliography, preserve the DOI and verify its exact author/volume/year fields against the publisher record rather than an inherited citation string. [Publisher page](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2021.764058/full).
